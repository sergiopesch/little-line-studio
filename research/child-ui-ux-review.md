# Calm drawing UI for ages 3–10

Research reviewed 25 October 2026. Scope: a supervised screen demonstration while the child draws on paper. This is an evidence-informed design review, not a systematic review, clinical intervention, or proof that this product improves learning. Primary experimental findings, accessibility standards, and professional guidance have different evidential weight.

## Findings and decisions

## Colour, texture, type and composition

There is no evidence in this review for a universally optimal colour, particular saturation, rounded typeface, or paper texture that improves drawing learning across ages 3–10. Avoid claims such as “blue scientifically calms children” or gender-based palettes.

The approved 26 October palette uses warm cream paper (`#FFFEFA`) outside a plain white demonstration area and dark ink text (`#17202F`). Pencil gold (`#F5CF60`) identifies primary actions, with dark labels and a visible ochre boundary (`#9A6B00`). Blue identifies selected/on states, progress and links; pale yellow (`#FFF4CC`) supports notes and acknowledgement reminders. Secondary controls stay quiet, More time remains prominent, and manual Next remains optional. Keep headline letters dark with their gold underline; gold text on paper is insufficiently legible. Shared semantic tokens in `src/styles.css` replace the former page-scoped gold and misleading mint name. Hover, active, disabled, focus and error roles remain explicit. This supersedes the earlier blue-action/mint-note direction while retaining the white teaching board. Keep subtle texture outside text and drawing strokes. These are coherent design choices, not established cognitive effects. Prefer whitespace and thin borders to decorative cards around every element. Rounded corners provide a friendly visual tone; contrast and legibility do the accessibility work.

Use self-hosted Nunito with a system-font fallback, readable body sizes, short lines, and generous spacing. The teacher's drawing should occupy the largest region. The cat reference must match the demonstrated structure, while copy welcomes different results. Decorative elements must never look like controls.

## Age-sensitive application

- **3–5:** an adult reads/operates as needed; large exploratory movements are enough; demonstrate on a separate piece of paper without taking over. No expectation of a faithful copy.
- **6–7:** one instruction at a time, with icons and readable labels; adult stays nearby and offers a hint on request.
- **8–10:** same uncluttered surface, respectful language, optional detail and creative variation; no infantile reward loops.

Age is a broad starting point. Motor ability, reading, sensory preferences and interests differ within each band. No birth date, name, account or stored age profile is needed.

## Sound, pacing and motion

The existing music evidence review reports mixed effects. At the user's request, music defaults on but lesson playback begins only from the supervising adult's Begin gesture; explicit sound checks use separate adult gestures. Keep a pre-start opt-out and visible in-session mute. The shared mix lowers music beneath teaching and silences it immediately for dialogs, help, pauses, navigation and hidden tabs. This is a product preference, not evidence that music improves learning. Do not describe a tempo, frequency or genre as scientifically optimal. Prepared narration may be unavailable; instructions must remain complete and readable. No microphone or camera is necessary.

Show one meaningful authored part, then provide a practice window matched to the movement. The later parent-started automatic flow supersedes this review's original compulsory wait after each mark. More time still holds indefinitely, and Pause, Replay and Back remain family-controlled. No silence detection, urgency, streaks or correctness score is used. A timeline gives an estimate rather than claiming readiness. Drawing movement illustrates how to make a mark; the separately approved home language banner has pause and reduced-motion support.

## Verification and open evidence gaps

Test the entire lesson, rapid input, pause during animation, replay, settings, hidden tabs, music mute, missing speech support, keyboard focus, reduced motion, 320px mobile reflow and desktop composition. Review headers, dependencies, external requests, storage and asset exposure. Measure production bundle size and browser loading/interaction indicators. [Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) distinguishes lab observations from real-user percentiles; local performance cannot establish global performance.

The next learning-validation step is supervised observation with families across all three age bands, including varied reading/motor and sensory needs. Look for mistaken taps, requests for help, voluntary stopping, frustration, and ability to try a related drawing later without the guide. Do not collect child recordings by default. Technical QA cannot establish developmental effectiveness, legal compliance, or universal usability.
