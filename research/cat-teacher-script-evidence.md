# Cat teacher pilot: script and pacing evidence

Updated 26 October 2026 for the current Cat teaching pack and shorter practice revision. This note records the limited factual basis for the animal content and the authored pacing choices. It does not establish that this app improves learning outcomes.

The spoken teacher explains the visible mark while tldraw demonstrates it: a large shape makes room for small features; positions and spaces make the face readable; light, overlapping colour marks build the coat; small areas call for smaller marks. Short on-screen captions name the immediate action. These are drawing explanations and design choices, rather than claims that one technique is required for a child's success. Different shapes, colours, incomplete marks and stopping remain welcome.

Only two animal facts are used, each beside the feature currently being drawn:

| Spoken fact | Source checked 26 October 2026 | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cats can turn their ears towards sounds. | Cats Protection, [5 facts about cat ears, The Cat, summer 2020, page 39](https://www.cats.org.uk/media/4573/039-thecat_summer20-five-facts.pdf). The charity explains that cats can move each ear to locate the direction of sound. | The lesson omits muscle counts, degree measurements and claims about an individual cat's mood. |
| Cats use their whiskers to sense nearby objects. | Cats Protection, [Why do cats have whiskers?](https://www.cats.org.uk/cats-blog/why-do-cats-have-whiskers), published 18 April 2024, section “Sensing objects up close”. | The lesson does not claim that a whisker guarantees a cat can fit through a gap, that all cats have the same count, or that a drawn face predicts an animal's feelings. |

The smiling mouth, simplified ears and three whiskers on either side are illustration choices, not anatomical instruction. The teacher calls the smile the picture's friendly mouth and welcomes other expressions. No interaction with a live animal is requested.

Earlier pilots used much longer practice windows. The current pack keeps its reviewed spoken teaching and patient native crayon demonstrations while shortening the wait afterwards. Observation has one second of practice, drawing windows are 2–6 seconds, and colouring windows are 1–6 seconds. Authored practice totals 62 seconds, and the default guided estimate is 293.430 seconds, excluding pauses, replay and open exploration. Colouring is split into a colour choice, the two halves of the coat, small details, optional patterns and reflection. This responds to family feedback about boredom without adding unrelated speech to fill gaps.

These timings are product hypotheses for this pilot. Actual recorded voice duration, demonstration duration and the selected speed determine the displayed estimate. Pause, Replay and unlimited More time are essential; time estimates are not learning deadlines. The voice does not infer a child's progress, evaluate their paper, or decide when they should continue.

The warm ginger, pink and cream example aims to relate the coloured tldraw picture to the soft, illustrated homepage references. It is an example palette: the spoken teacher explicitly welcomes the child's colours, white spaces and optional patterns. The six colouring IDs are `colour-choice`, `colour-fur`, `colour-fur-right`, `colour-details`, `colour-patterns` and `colour-reflection`; only the four painting steps have demonstrations.

An educator/child observation study, device listening review and assessment of transfer to independent drawing remain outstanding. The narrow animal-care sources above support the two cat facts; they do not validate the app's pacing, voice, music, learning efficacy or educational suitability for every child.
