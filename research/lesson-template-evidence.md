# Evidence and limits for the reusable teaching packs

Initially reviewed 26 October 2026 for the Cat, Friendly sun and Rainbow teaching packs. The template is a practical authoring contract for this app; it is not a validated curriculum or a claim that one script, music choice, crayon movement or fixed duration is best for all children. The curriculum has since expanded to 30 complete story-and-crayon packs; see the [project overview](../README.md) for the current catalogue. See also [the authoring template](../DRAWING_LESSON_TEMPLATE.md), [drawing evidence](drawing-instruction-evidence.md) and [scripted lesson evidence](scripted-lesson-evidence.md).

## Observation before, during and after drawing

Vlach and Carver's original 2008 report studied 22 kindergarten children in one laboratory school, randomly divided between observation coaching and a control condition. Coaching encouraged looking before, during and after drawing; coached children improved more on the researchers' drawing scores. The small, selected sample and researcher-created scoring limit generalisation; this does not establish durable independent transfer or validate our automatic screen teacher. We use a useful opening noticing cue, a few relevant references to position/space while demonstrating, and an optional final look. We do not reproduce the study's live tutor, multi-angle objects, comprehension questions or observational measurement. [Original author report](https://ecrp.illinois.edu/v10n1/vlach.html).

The existing research notes distinguish drawing trials from adjacent work on segmentation, modelling, scaffolding and process feedback. Their findings motivate clear meaningful turns, requested help and family control; they do not establish the exact second counts, narration lengths or concurrent timing used here. Shorter practice after teaching responds to this family's report of boredom, while patient native demonstrations remain visible. Cat and Sun each have 62 seconds of authored practice, with default estimates of 293.430 and 262.895 seconds respectively. These are product hypotheses requiring observation, not evidence that all Basic drawings should share one duration. Standard outline practice defaults of 12/18/24 seconds for Basic/Intermediate/Expert, plus a demonstration-complexity allowance, are equally provisional. ## Sun facts and illustration choices

NASA identifies the Sun as a star and describes its light and heat reaching Earth. The sun opening uses only these simple points. Its outward drawn rays are an illustration convention for light; they are not a diagram of eight physical spokes. The smiling face, yellow/orange palette and imaginary garden are explicitly storybook choices. We make no claim that the Sun has eyes, a mouth or a child's preferred emotional expression. [NASA Sun facts](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/), [NASA Space Place: All About the Sun](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/all-about-the-sun/en/).

Observation uses the prepared screen drawing. The requested observation hint says never to look directly at the real Sun, rather than inviting live solar observation. NASA's child-facing material also warns against staring at the Sun. No telescope, filter or outdoor observation activity is part of this drawing lesson. [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/all-about-the-sun/en/).

The story thread connects a circle, outward lines, warm example colours and an invitation to draw an imaginary garden. It provides coherence and subject interest. The two source facts support only the astronomy statements; they do not establish that storytelling improves drawing outcomes or that these colours improve mood or attention.

## Crayon technique and music

The native tldraw crayon gesture is a demonstration choice: small back-and-forth strokes, light overlap, visible lifts, retained paper grain and a limited second colour layer. These are concrete techniques a child may try on paper, not a developmental test or a requirement to stay within a boundary. Different colours, gaps, marks, omitted details and stopping remain acceptable. Technical renderer checks can establish stroke sequencing, source identity and completion gates; they cannot establish expert artistic quality, comfortable physical grip or suitability for every child's motor access needs.

Music is a separate optional atmosphere choice. Per-subject identity means a reviewed fixed asset and an original restrained musical brief, not a learning claim. At the user's request, music is enabled by default but lesson playback begins only with the supervising adult's Begin gesture; pre-start sound checks require their own explicit gesture. The adult can opt out before beginning or mute during the lesson. Music yields in level to speech through the shared mix; immediate interruption silence and parent controls remain. This default is a product preference, not a conclusion from the mixed, task-dependent research or an app-specific finding of improved drawing learning. [Music evidence](background-music-evidence.md).

## Acceptance boundaries

Keep three kinds of evidence separate: source support for factual statements; technical/browser evidence that authored media, native drawing, timing and controls operate as intended; and educator/family evidence about comprehensibility, interest, comfort and independent drawing. A full real-time browser run can reveal accumulated waits or missing demonstrations without showing that a child learned. Device listening, child pace observations, later independent use and inclusive educator review remain necessary before stronger educational claims.

The app still cannot see or judge paper, collect child audio or choose settings through a model. Supervision acknowledgement remains a local adult declaration rather than verified identity or consent. These teaching packs add no new child-data collection, provider interaction during playback or legal-compliance guarantee.

Balloon extends the same template with a pretend paper celebration, a rounded shape/knot/string relationship, a white shine and optional bow/pattern. It introduces no additional science or developmental claim. Its short practice windows follow the family feedback about unnecessary waits; they are authoring choices, not experimentally established learning optima. The teacher colouring is checked using native tldraw pressure geometry, width, grain offsets, partial caps and the alternative solid zoom mode against independent subject boundaries. This technical containment review does not judge a child's paper or demand matching neatness.
