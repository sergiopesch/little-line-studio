# Background music during children's drawing and learning

Research checked 25 October 2026. Scoped investigation of background listening, not music lessons, musical training, or clinical music therapy. Four primary empirical sources below were opened as full reports or an author-institution abstract. Findings concern different tasks and ages; they do not establish background music as an effective drawing pedagogy for ages 3–10.

## Current product decision and earlier research recommendation

Treat music as an optional atmosphere preference, never a promised learning enhancement. The original research proposal favoured off by default and full muting during instruction. The user's later preference supersedes that product proposal: music now defaults on, but starts only when the supervising adult chooses Begin or explicitly checks sound. It ducks beneath teaching instead of stopping at each ordinary step. Pre-start opt-out, a visible in-session mute and immediate silence for pauses, dialogs, help, navigation and hidden tabs remain essential. The browser has no microphone capture.

The current default and mix are product preferences informed by family feedback, not an experimentally validated optimal audio policy. The evidence below remains mixed and has not become stronger because the setting changed. Do not claim a scientifically optimal tempo, genre, frequency, volume, or playlist. Neither “classical” nor “lo-fi” is a guarantee of concentration or creativity.

## 1. Most directly relevant preschool experiment cautions against added audio

**Sarı, Başal, Takacs & Bus (2019). A randomized controlled trial to test efficacy of digital enhancements of storybooks in support of narrative comprehension and word learning.** DOI: 10.1016/j.jecp.2018.11.006. [Author-institution abstract, opened](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/a-randomized-controlled-trial-to-test-efficacy-of-digital-enhance/); [publisher abstract and method excerpts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022096518304636).

- N=99 typically developing children, ages 4–6, two public kindergartens in Bursa, Turkey; randomised control trial with control and four combinations of static/animated pictures and music/sounds.
- Children encountered two storybooks twice in groups of 2–3. Music and background sounds did not improve story comprehension and negatively affected receptive vocabulary. Visual enhancements improved comprehension.
- Crucial limitation: music and environmental sounds were bundled. This does not isolate instrumental background music or show every sound is harmful. A narrated story is not independent drawing.
- Relevance: direct warning against assuming extra audio improves a young child's learning while spoken content is already present. Supports testing quiet instruction rather than adding music automatically.
- Access: conclusions and sample verified from the institution-hosted original abstract; full statistical report was not available through that page. No effect size asserted here.

## 2. Frequently cited positive classroom findings are small and mostly older

**Hallam, Price & Katsarou (2002). The Effects of Background Music on Primary School Pupils' Task Performance.** DOI: 10.1080/03055690220124551. [Original report, opened](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2002-hallam.pdf).

- Study 1: N=31 ages 10–11, one London-area class, random allocation to music/non-music groups, four 15-minute arithmetic sessions. Music was selected as calming by children. It increased work rate; accuracy did not significantly improve. Individual-day significance was inconsistent.
- Study 2: N=30 ages 11–12, randomised across silence, pleasant/calming music, and unpleasant/arousing music, ten per condition. Cued sentence recall means were 4.9, 6.9, and 3.3 respectively.
- Limits: small samples, short tasks, particular recordings; most children fall outside the product's age range. The first study is about speed, not improved understanding. Different recordings differ on multiple musical dimensions; mechanism cannot be attributed solely to tempo or arousal. Hypothetical altruism responses are not proof of real prosocial behaviour.
- Relevance: effects can depend on task and music; results do not justify continuous music, a universal “calming playlist,” or preschool learning claims.

## 3. Child auditory-distraction evidence: relevant mechanism, not music evidence

**Leist, Lachmann & Klatte (2025). Impact of irrelevant speech and non-speech sounds on serial recall of verbal and spatial items in children and adults.** DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-85855-w. [Original full report, opened](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11732993/).

- N=137 children ages 7 years 9 months–11 years 3 months and N=98 adults. Different participants performed verbal versus spatial serial-recall tasks; each experienced silence, unfamiliar Danish speech, and environmental sounds. Verbal-task children N=70; spatial-task children N=67.
- Both sounds impaired verbal recall; speech was more disruptive. Spatial recall disruption occurred in third graders, not fourth graders or adults. No habituation appeared over the experimental trials.
- Limits: music was not experimentally tested. A short memory task is not drawing; children's sound effects were tested over only eight trials per condition. Age and school grade were strongly correlated, preventing clean separation.
- Relevance: remove competing audio when retaining instructions. Avoid presuming that visually oriented tasks are immune or that children inevitably “get used to it.” Individual variability and task dependence support a readily available silent option.

## 4. Lyrics versus instrumental: useful adult evidence, explicitly indirect

**Souza & Barbosa (2023). Should We Turn off the Music? Music with Lyrics Interferes with Cognitive Tasks.** DOI: 10.5334/joc.273. [Original full report, opened](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10162369/).

- University-student online within-participant experiment: 136 enrolled; analysed task samples N=113 verbal recall, N=118 visual recall, N=123 reading, N=106 arithmetic after task-specific missing-data/exclusion rules. Demographic respondents were ages 18–58, mean20.74. No children studied.
- Conditions were ambient silence, instrumental lo-fi, and Portuguese lyrical music. Lyrics reduced memory and reading performance; instrumental comparisons were often inconclusive. Arithmetic performance showed evidence against an effect.
- Limits: adult extrapolation; different genres/recordings mean lyrics are not the sole isolated difference. Remote ambient listening and attrition limit control. Subjective benefit did not always align with performance.
- Relevance: instrumental music is a less linguistically competing candidate to test; it is not established as beneficial or universally harmless. Child preference and parent perception cannot substitute for measured outcomes.

## Evidence gaps and individual differences

This search did not establish a high-quality replicated trial showing improved learning to draw from instrumental background music across ages 3–10. Preschool drawing preferences, music-making interventions, and adult divergent-thinking findings cannot fill that gap. Preference for an entertaining drawing app is distinct from improved drawing skill, retention, creative independence, or wellbeing.

Auditory sensitivity, language comprehension, hearing/access needs, fatigue, familiarity, and task difficulty should inform parent choices and user testing. The reviewed experiments do not establish an automatic classifier or clinical music recommendation for these differences. Do not label a child from sound preferences or infer diagnoses. Let families select silence without a degraded experience.

## Current playback contract

| Application state | Music behaviour |
|---|---|
| Page load, catalogue or supervision confirmation | No automatic playback; an explicit adult sound check may preview it |
| Adult chooses Begin after acknowledgement | Start the selected prepared track unless music was turned off |
| Teacher instruction or demonstration narration | Lower to 32% of selected gain; recover gently afterwards |
| Help, parent settings or another dialog | Silence immediately |
| Independent practice or open exploration | Continue the same optional instrumental track without restarting |
| Mute, Pause, stop, hidden tab or safety interruption | Silence immediately |

Audio state belongs to application code, with the voice model unable to enable it. Preferences remain in page memory and reset on reload; the default does not bypass the adult-start boundary. Use locally served or appropriately licensed tracks without ads, external autoplay recommendations, or additional third-party tracking. Do not send drawing or child audio to choose music. These privacy/cost choices are architecture decisions, not findings from the music studies.

## Validation before claiming benefits

Compare optional music with silence during drawing only, keeping instruction audio identical and uncontested. Counterbalance track/task order and account for parent assistance. Track requests for repetition, independent transfer, delayed retention, child enjoyment, parent workload, and voluntary muting separately. Ask about sensory comfort without medical labelling. Analyse age and individual variation rather than only a pooled average. Predefine an acceptable lack of learning benefit: music may remain an optional enjoyable feature if it does not impair learning or comfort. Cost and enjoyment alone cannot establish educational effectiveness.
